The pilot is agreed. The clinicians are keen. Then procurement sends the DTAC form, and the supplier finds out how much evidence it doesn't have.

Since 6 April 2026, that form is the refreshed DTAC, and the old version should no longer be used \[1\].

## What changed

NHS England says the new form is 25% shorter, with questions that duplicated the DSPT and other processes removed. The five areas stay: clinical safety (C1), data protection (C2), technical security (C3), interoperability (C4), and usability and accessibility (D1). Scope now follows NICE's definition of digital health technologies: software, including software that runs with hardware, but not hardware or embedded firmware \[1\].

## What suppliers must now show

- **Clinical safety (C1):** a decision tree for whether the product is a medical device and whether DCB0129 or DCB0160 applies. A medical device also triggers the Pre-Acquisition Questionnaire. NHS-specific Clinical Safety Officer training is no longer required \[1\].
- **Data protection (C2):** a DPIA on NHS England's template, privacy notices and terms, with international transfers referenced to the IDTA and a transfer risk assessment. Lower-risk products can skip some questions \[1\].
- **Technical security (C3):** confirmation against the Software Security Code of Practice, and MFA on administrator and remote-access accounts. Signatories of the Cyber Security Supply Chain Charter skip some checks \[1\].
- **Interoperability (C4):** a justification of your APIs and data standards against NHS guidance, and proper NHS Number verification \[1\].
- **Accessibility (D1):** WCAG 2.2 AA and the Accessible Information Standard. This area is no longer scored \[1\].

## What it doesn't fix

There is still no central pass. Each procurement generally asks for its own DTAC; a shared repository is only being explored \[1\]. DTAC also sits alongside the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, not instead of it. For 2026/27, version 9 applies, with a baseline submission due by 31 December 2026 and the final one by 30 June 2027 \[2\]. Where DCB0129 applies, a Clinical Safety Officer still reviews the hazard log and safety case report \[3\].

## Build the evidence once

The suppliers who move fastest treat DTAC as part of the product, not a form:

- keep one evidence pack, versioned with the product
- write the intended purpose first, because the DPIA and the safety case depend on it
- run the DSPT on the same calendar every year
- re-check C1 whenever a feature changes what the product does

## Frequently asked questions

### Is DTAC mandatory?

NHS buyers generally ask for a DTAC form in each procurement of digital health technology \[1\].

### Does DTAC replace the DSPT?

No. Overlapping questions were removed so the two work together \[1\].

### Do we still need a Clinical Safety Officer?

If DCB0129 applies, yes: a CSO reviews the safety case report and hazard log. What DTAC dropped is the requirement for NHS-specific CSO training \[1\]\[3\].
